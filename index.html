Race-day workflow
  1. 8:00 am Melbourne: Consult official acceptances/scratchings and track updates; verify the expected active field for any races you want to study. This is a planning checkpoint, not a guarantee that official updates have been released.
  2. 60 minutes before the scheduled jump: Review relevant form and write down sources. Keep uncertain ratings blank. Choose a race; you do not need to analyse all ten.
  3. 30 minutes before: Recheck withdrawals, late rider changes, track, and a single bookmaker's fixed-win prices for every active runner. Record one coherent, timestamped odds snapshot.
  4. 15 minutes before: Review missing information and the market quality warning. Freeze a pre-race forecast only if its assumptions are defensible; log optional paper-only stakes within the fixed budget. If the field or odds are incomplete, skip this race.
  5. At the scheduled jump: Stop editing predictions for the experiment. Ignore unsupported last-minute tips and do not chase price moves. Actual running times can change; this app does not track delays.
  6. After the official result: Verify the winner from the racing authority, record the source, settle any paper bets, and compare hybrid versus market Brier/log loss.
  7. End of meeting: Export a fresh JSON backup. Review the number of prospective forecasts, data gaps, and performance—do not optimize sliders against today's outcomes.

Scheduled times are not live start confirmations. Sources are external, and RaceLab does not scrape or automatically ingest their content. This is a paper-only learning tool, not a betting recommendation.
